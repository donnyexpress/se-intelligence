# Karagiannis et al. — A Framework for Structuring Research Campaigns with MBSE

## TL;DR
Shoal Group (Karagiannis, Wylie) and Turen (Liddy) propose using MBSE not for known-system design, but for *ambitious R&D where the system isn't defined yet*. Start from an end-state concept, derive research questions, run prototypes against the questions, evolve the architecture as knowledge grows.

## Why it matters
The paper inverts the usual MBSE framing: instead of "model the system we know we want," it's "model the system we're trying to discover." This is exactly what early-stage R&D programs struggle with — they want MBSE discipline but don't have a stable system to model. The framework gives them a way in.

## Key ideas
- The end-state concept is described at high level; not as a fixed requirement, but as a target to discover the path to.
- Research questions are derived from the concept, and each question is answered by a specific prototype.
- The architecture is incrementally developed by each prototype's results, not designed up front.
- MBSE provides traceability: which research question motivated which prototype, and which architecture decision came from which prototype's results.
- The framework is designed for "ambitious use cases that may otherwise be unattainable" — exactly where traditional V-model SE falls down.

## My take
This is a quietly important paper. Most MBSE writing assumes the system is at least roughly known. The Shoal work addresses the gap where the system *is* the research question, which is most of the interesting work in early-stage programs. If your team does TRL 1–5 work and has been told "we'll do MBSE later, when we know what we're building," this framework is the answer to that.
