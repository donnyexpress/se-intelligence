# Navigating Complex Systems: A review of Systems Practice Frameworks

## TL;DR

Systems engineering methodologies are complex and interconnected, reflecting various philosophies and problem-solving approaches. Practitioners often question their suitability for all contexts, especially complex adaptive systems. This has led to the need for meta-methodologies” which could help in the selection of appropriate methodologies. Michael C. Jackson’s “Critical Systems Thinking: A Practitioners Guide” introduces a more pluralistic approach, exploring different systemic perspectives to inform the selection and combination of methodologies, replacing the term meta-methodology with the “Practice” term as in Critical Systems Practice (CSP). Taking this lead this paper reviews a set of similarly constructed publications, termed Systems Practice Frameworks (SPF), that all seek to aid the reader in characterizing their unique problem and proposing pointers to tools, techniques, heuristics or methodologies. It compares and contrast these approaches to assess their suitability to guide readers in selecting suitable approaches to address Complex Adaptive Systems and identify recommendations for improving SPFs. It concludes that SPFs are increasingly pivotal in helping Systems Engineers face their unique complex challenges. CSP is identified as the most useful SPF, but the other SPF’s indicate improvements that can be made, such as simplification of the advice, and pointing towards a wider accessible range of practices.
