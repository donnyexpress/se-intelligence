# Unewisse et al. — Early-Stage Digital Engineering for Energy Decarbonization

## TL;DR
Shoal Group's Paper #180 presents a digital engineering approach for complex energy decarbonization (CED) projects, aimed at small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) — integrating MBSE with OpenModelica and open operations research tools to do whole-of-system feasibility and risk analysis.

## Why it matters
Most MBSE work targets large aerospace/defense programs with big tooling budgets. Energy decarbonization is a different beast — many small projects, SME consultancies, and tight economics. This paper shows MBSE being adapted to that context, with open tools, in a sector that's about to see trillions in investment.

## Key ideas
- The framework integrates three tool classes: MBSE (architecture, traceability), OpenModelica (physical/behavioural simulation), and open OR tools (optimization, scenario analysis).
- The deliverable is not a model, it's *advice* — SMEs sell decarbonization roadmaps to project owners, and the digital engineering backs the advice with evidence.
- The economics drive the tooling choices: a SME can't afford a 6-figure-per-year MBSE seat license, so the framework is built around open tools.
- Risk analysis is done in the same environment as feasibility — one model, multiple views, rather than separate models for separate stakeholders.

## My take
The most useful thing in this paper isn't the framework, it's the *economic framing*. The authors are explicit: this is for SMEs selling advice, not primes building systems. That changes everything about how you structure the model, what you prioritize, and what you leave out. If you're doing energy transition work and your team is smaller than a major prime, this is the closest thing to a recipe you'll find.
