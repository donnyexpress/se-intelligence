# The Role of Human Factors Engineering in Rapid Prototyping

## TL;DR
A chapter talk on integrating human factors engineering (HFE) into rapid prototyping cycles. The talk argues that HFE is often left until late in the prototype cycle, when it's expensive to incorporate — and proposes an approach where HFE is built into the prototype loop from day one.

## Why it matters
Most rapid prototyping efforts focus on the technical system and treat human factors as a late-stage gate. The talk gives practical patterns for bringing HFE into the loop earlier, with examples from medical device and consumer hardware prototyping.

## Key ideas
- HFE includes physical ergonomics, cognitive ergonomics, accessibility, training, and documentation. All five are usually left until late in the prototype cycle.
- The cost of fixing a human-factors issue after the prototype is much higher than fixing it during the prototype. The talk gives specific cost multipliers.
- Practical patterns for early HFE: include an HFE reviewer in the daily standup, run quick ergonomics tests on the first mockup, use accessibility checklists as design constraints not verification gates.
- The talk is honest about where the patterns don't work: highly regulated industries where verification is a separate phase.

## So what
If you run a prototyping team that builds physical or interactive systems, this talk gives you a practical template for bringing HFE into the loop. If you're in a regulated industry, the patterns still apply, but the verification handoff needs to be explicit.

## My take
The talk is short and practical. The biggest contribution is the cost-multiplier framing — making the case that early HFE is cheaper, with real numbers. If you're pitching HFE integration to a budget holder, this talk gives you the data.
