# MBSE patterns in practice — IS 2024 (Dublin)

## TL;DR
Industrial panel from IS 2024 surveying MBSE pattern adoption across automotive, defense, and rail. The convergence: layered architecture models, scenario-driven verification, and traceability as a queryable artifact. Three years of data, real numbers, not vision decks.

## Why it matters
If you're deciding whether to invest in MBSE patterns vs. document-centric SE, this panel has the kind of field data you can't get from vendor whitepapers. Includes the failure modes as well as the wins.

## Key ideas
- Layered architecture (requirements → logical → physical) is the dominant pattern in 2024 deployments.
- Scenarios drive verification definitions, not the other way around.
- Tooling matters less than the modelling conventions you commit to across the team.
- The biggest predictor of MBSE success: an MBSE champion with line-management authority.
- Polyglot MBSE (multiple tools) almost never pays off. Pick one, standardize, train.

## My take
The "MBSE champion with line-management authority" point is the most actionable. Most failed MBSE rollouts had champions, but the champions were either (a) consultants without authority or (b) architects without people-management authority. The pattern works when the champion can both set the technical standard *and* enforce it.
