# Resilient systems engineering — IW 2024 (Torrance)

## TL;DR
Workshop papers from IW 2024 on resilience engineering for cyber-physical systems. The unifying theme: resilience is a property of the *system under operation*, not a property you can bolt on at integration time.

## Why it matters
Resilience — the ability to maintain critical function under adverse conditions — is increasingly the design goal for systems in energy, transport, healthcare, and defense. The IW 2024 papers give a synthesis of the state of practice across these domains.

## Key ideas
- Resilience ≠ reliability. Reliability is "no failures under expected conditions." Resilience is "continued acceptable operation under unexpected conditions."
- The "graceful degradation" pattern is the most consistent success across domains.
- Adaptive capacity (the ability to reconfigure) is a stronger resilience lever than redundancy.
- Operational resilience requires continuous monitoring of leading indicators, not just lagging ones (failures, outages).

## My take
Resilience is where the SE community is most under-investing. Most teams still design for "things work" rather than "things continue to provide value when conditions deteriorate." The IW 2024 work is useful precisely because it gives you language and patterns for the "continue to provide value" framing.
