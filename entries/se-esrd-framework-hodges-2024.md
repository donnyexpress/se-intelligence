# A Framework for SE in Early-Stage R&D

## TL;DR
A framework from the INCOSE LA chapter that adapts systems thinking to early-stage R&D portfolios — pre-program, pre-product work where the goal is discovering what to build, not building it. The framework distinguishes four R&D stages and what systems engineering looks like in each.

## Why it matters
Most SE frameworks are written for programs that already know what they're building. Early-stage R&D is a different game: high uncertainty, multiple parallel bets, and success measured by learning rate, not delivery rate. The INCOSE LA chapter's framework is one of the first to take this seriously.

## Key ideas
- Four R&D stages: exploration, concept validation, prototype demonstration, technology maturation. Each has different metrics, different artifacts, and different system-engineering practices.
- Systems thinking shows up *earlier* than traditional SE — in the exploration stage, you model the problem space, not the solution.
- Portfolios, not programs. Early-stage R&D is a portfolio of bets, not a single program. The framework proposes portfolio-level metrics and review cadences.
- Stakeholders include researchers, technologists, and the eventual customer — the customer in R&D is often abstract.
- A model of "the right answer at the right time" replaces the traditional "the right answer." In R&D, the answer is provisional; the value is in how quickly you can update it.

## So what
If you work in a research org or an R&D-heavy unit, this is one of the few SE frameworks that actually fits your day. If you work in a program, the framework's vocabulary for distinguishing "are we still in R&D or are we now in program execution?" is useful even if you never run R&D yourself.

## My take
The framework's biggest contribution is naming the stages and giving them distinct practices. Most R&D orgs accidentally apply program-stage practices (full requirements docs, formal reviews) to R&D-stage work, which slows learning. The framework gives you a vocabulary to push back on that.
