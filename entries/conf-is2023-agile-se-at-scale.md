# Agile SE at scale — IS 2023 (Honolulu)

## TL;DR
Industry experience reports from IS 2023 on applying agile practices to large-scale system development. The shared finding: agile works in SE, but only with deliberate adaptation — the unmodified Scrum/SAFe playbook fails on hardware-in-the-loop programs.

## Why it matters
The "is agile compatible with SE?" debate has been running for a decade. This IS 2023 session series gave the clearest picture yet of what works and what doesn't, with data from aerospace, automotive, and medical device programs.

## Key ideas
- Agile cadence on the *system* level is incompatible with hardware-in-the-loop. Asynchronous integration testing is the workaround.
- "Agile SE" is not "agile development with more paperwork." The ceremonies serve different purposes in SE: design reviews, integration gates, certification evidence.
- The most common failure: trying to run 2-week sprints on a 36-month hardware program. The cadence has to be nested.
- Hybrid models (V-model with agile inside each phase) are the practical norm at scale.

## My take
The IS community has moved from "can we be agile in SE?" to "how do we tailor agile to SE?" The latter is the productive question. If your team is wrestling with whether to "go agile" in SE, the answer is yes, but you'll need to design the cadence and ceremonies for your specific system type. The textbook answer is wrong.
