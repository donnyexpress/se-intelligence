# IW 2025 (Seville) — INCOSE Working Group outcomes

## TL;DR
INCOSE's International Workshop 2025 in Seville drew 658 attendees from 35 countries (384 in-person, 274 virtual) for working-group sessions across the discipline. No papers — the IW format is working sessions, not conference talks. The output is what each Working Group advances.

## Why it matters
The IW is where INCOSE's discipline-advancement work actually happens. The IS is the public showcase; the IW is where Working Groups meet, draft, and decide. Tracking IW outcomes is how you know where the discipline is heading before it shows up at the next IS.

## Key ideas
- Resilient Systems Working Group (RSWG) used IW 2025 to advance its 2025 cycle-1 SEBoK update on resilience. They reviewed advances in AI, MBSE, digital engineering with digital twins, and organizational resilience — and updated their "Resilience Elevator Speech" and "How to Justify Resilience" documents.
- Configuration Management WG, Embedding SE in Organizations WG, Sustainability WG, PM-SE Integration WG, Human Systems Integration WG, Requirements WG all met and produced WG-level progress notes (not public papers).
- The OpenMBEE community ran a half-day session on SysML v2, including a JPL Digital Engineering keynote, Boeing MMS deployment report, and graph-metric model complexity analysis.
- Cross-WG coordination: RSWG, FuSE (Future of SE), and ADAPTSEM coordinating on the "Justify Resilience" narrative.

## My take
The IW is under-publicized. If you only follow IS proceedings, you miss 80% of where the discipline is moving. The trick is to follow *individual Working Group* outputs (their newsletters, their SEBoK contributions, their public reports) — that's where the actual discipline work shows up between the biennial symposia. Three to watch: RSWG for resilience, FuSE for the 2035 SE vision, and MBSE Initiative for SysML v2 migration.
