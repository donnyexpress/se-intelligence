# SE Maturity and Cost Modeling Framework for GenAI

## TL;DR
A framework from the INCOSE San Diego chapter that combines SE process maturity assessment (à la CMMI) with cost modeling for GenAI-augmented SE work. The paper is a working draft — the framework is not yet standardized — but it gives SE managers a starting point for "how do we measure whether our GenAI investments are paying off?"

## Why it matters
Most organizations adopting GenAI in SE are doing it on faith, not measurement. The paper gives you a way to assess your current SE process maturity, map GenAI capabilities to maturity levels, and model the cost implications of each.

## Key ideas
- A maturity model for SE processes (inspired by CMMI but adapted for modern, GenAI-augmented practice) with five levels.
- For each maturity level, a set of GenAI capabilities that are practical and safe to introduce. The model says: don't introduce capability X until your process is at level Y.
- A cost model for GenAI in SE work: licensing, infrastructure, integration, training, ongoing evaluation. The cost model distinguishes "cheap to try" from "expensive to do badly."
- The framework is presented as a working draft. The author invites feedback and case studies.

## So what
If you're a SE manager or process lead, this is a starting framework for "where do we start with GenAI in our SE work, and how do we know if it's working." It's not prescriptive — the paper is honest that the field is moving too fast for a standard — but it's a useful scaffold.

## My take
The paper's most useful contribution is the *sequencing* — it says don't introduce GenAI into a process that isn't mature enough to handle it. Most organizations skip that step and end up with brittle automations on top of immature processes. The framework gives you a way to have that conversation with leadership.
