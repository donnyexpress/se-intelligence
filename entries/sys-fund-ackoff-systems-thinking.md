# Ackoff — Systems Thinking for the 21st Century

## TL;DR
Russell Ackoff's framing distinguishes systems thinking from analysis: a system is a whole that cannot be understood by taking it apart, because the properties of the parts depend on the whole.

## Why it matters
This is the foundational text for anyone working in SE who keeps getting asked "but what does 'systems' actually mean?" Ackoff separates four kinds of problems — absolutes, comparatives, optimizations, and design problems — and argues that SE practitioners almost always work on the last kind. Worth reading once a year.

## Key ideas
- A system is an entity that maintains its existence through the interaction of its parts.
- Doing the wrong thing right (optimization) is not the same as doing the right thing (design).
- Systems thinking requires synthesis before analysis, not after.
- Most engineering failures are design failures dressed up as implementation failures.
